Research

Research

My research has two lines: (1) the politicization on social media and digital platforms; and (2) the organizing dynamics of online communities in the digital ecosystem. They often intersect with each other, such as when membership overlap between political and non-political communities fuels politicization, or when international platform migration becomes a form of alternative political expression. I use computational and AI-assisted methods, surveys, content analysis, and interviews to study online communities on platforms such as Reddit, RedNote, and TikTok. My work has been published or accepted in journals including New Media & Society, Political Communication, and Journal of Information Technology & Politics.

Yibin Fan*, Benjamin Mako Hill, & Patricia Moy

This article shows political discussion in non-political digital contexts is even more likely to contain policy opinion expression than in explicitly political contexts, while the level of incivility does not significantly differ.

Published

“TikTok refugees”: motivations and political correlates of international platform migration

Yibin Fan* & Wang-Hai Tucker

This article conceptualizes international platform migration as a form of alternative political expression. Using a survey of platform migrants from TikTok to RedNote during the 2025 “TikTok Refugees” crisis, it shows that cultural openness and political expression were salient motivations for migration, and that migration was associated with lower levels of political trust, media trust, and political efficacy.

Published

The politics of the “non-political”: A conceptual review

Yufan Guo† & Yibin Fan*†

This review traces how theorists and empirical researchers in political science and communication have conceptualized what's viewed non-political. It reports two distinct conceptual understandings of the non-political, one is institutional and the other is critical, and they each inform different conceptualization approaches in empirical studies and report different relationships between the political and the non-political.

Political talk in non-political digital spaces

Yibin Fan*

This study reviews work on political talk in non-political digital spaces across post-positivist, interpretivist, and critical traditions. It identifies key concepts used to study this growing set of phenomena and calls for future research that can better connect these epistemological approaches.
